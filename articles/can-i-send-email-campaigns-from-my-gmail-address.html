Can I Send Email Campaigns from My Gmail Address?

Updated June 2026
Back to Email Marketing

If you have been using a personal Gmail address as your main business email, you will need to set up a domain-based email address before sending campaigns through Alvéya. This guide explains why, and walks you through your options.

The short version

When you send an email campaign through Alvéya, your emails are sent using a dedicated sending domain that has been authenticated for your account. This is what allows your emails to reach inboxes reliably rather than landing in spam.

The From address you use in your campaigns needs to match (or closely align with) that sending domain. If your From address is a personal Gmail account, such as yourname@gmail.com, your emails will almost certainly be rejected or sent to spam. This is not a limitation of Alvéya — it is how email authentication works globally, and Google tightened these rules significantly in 2024.

⚠️  Sending campaigns from a @gmail.com, @hotmail.com, or @yahoo.com address is likely to cause delivery failures. Most inbox providers will reject or filter these emails before they ever reach your subscribers.

Why this happens

When you send an email, inbox providers check whether the email was actually sent by who it claims to be from. This is done through a set of authentication checks called SPF, DKIM, and DMARC.

If your From address is yourname@gmail.com but the email was sent through Alvéya's infrastructure rather than Google's servers, the authentication check fails. Google's own security policies mean that emails claiming to be from a Gmail address but sent through a third party will be blocked or rejected.

A domain-based email address — such as yourname@yourbusiness.com — solves this completely. When your sending domain and your From address use the same domain, everything aligns and your emails have the best possible chance of reaching the inbox.

💡  You do not need to actively monitor the email address you use as your From address. It just needs to exist and be associated with your domain. You can set a separate reply-to address if you prefer replies to go somewhere else.

Setting up a domain-based email address

There are several ways to set this up, depending on what works best for you and your budget.

💡  Not sure where your domain is registered? Check with whoever manages your website. They will be able to point you toward the right email hosting option.

Once you have your domain email address

Once your domain-based email address is set up, you will add it to your campaign settings inside Alvéya so it appears as the From address in everything you send.

Before you start:  Make sure your dedicated sending domain has already been set up in Alvéya. If you are unsure whether this is done, check with your account setup confirmation email or contact support.

  1. Log in to your Alvéya account.
  2. Go to Settings in the left-hand navigation.
  3. Select Email Services, then navigate to the From Addresses section.
  4. Click Add From Address and enter your new domain email address.
  5. You may be asked to verify the address — follow the prompts to confirm ownership.
  6. Once verified, set this address as the default sender for your campaigns.

💡  If you plan to use this address as a genuine inbox as well, make sure you check it regularly. Replies from subscribers and auto-responses from inbox providers will land there.

In summary

A domain-based email address is not optional when it comes to email campaigns — it is the foundation that makes reliable delivery possible. The good news is that it is a straightforward thing to set up, and once it is done, you will not need to think about it again.

If you are unsure which option is right for you, the most common choice for practitioners is Google Workspace. It keeps everything familiar and gives you a professional address you can use across all of your business communications — not just for campaigns.

With your domain email address in place, your campaigns are set up to reach your subscribers' inboxes reliably.
